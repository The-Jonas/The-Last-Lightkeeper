Field of view panel

Second page of the tweak panel — press \ to change page. Every row is live: the game applies the value at once, and the game keeps it for the next run.

The values are saved. Every change goes to config/lighting.json, 0.8 seconds after you stop moving a slider, and at once when you close the panel. The next run reads that file back.

The file is written OVER the built-in defaults, one key at a time. A missing key, a wrong type or a value that is not a finite number is ignored, and that one field keeps its factory value. So the starting point is always the defaults in PlayerVisionParams and LightMaskParams — and deleting the file resets the game to factory values.

scripts\deploy.bat puts the file in the bundle when it exists, so a tuned build ships with your values.
The one rule behind all of it. The cone is not a torch. It says where the character LOOKS, not what the character SEES. What the player sees is the product of two things:
seen = GEOMETRY × LIGHT
│
├─ GEOMETRY = inside the cone, or inside the circle at the feet
└─ LIGHT    = the larger of: light that reaches the point,
              and: how near the character is (a straight ramp)
Where seen is 0 the picture stays monochrome and objects are invisible. Where it is 1 the colour comes back in full. So the far end of the cone goes grey when no light is there, and an object in the dark fades out with distance instead of popping into view.

How the panel is laid out Como o painel esta arrumado

The rows are grouped under titles and flow into as many COLUMNS as the window can hold, so nothing is squeezed into one tall strip any more. A group is never split across two columns.

Every yes/no row is a CHECKBOX and the whole row is clickable — you do not have to hit the small square. The same rows also answer the + and - keys when the row has focus. The arrow keys move the focus and skip the group titles. \ opens and closes the panel, P changes page, K changes the light shape.

0 · The camera how much map fits on screen

One row, at the top of the page. It sets the BASE zoom: the value the camera always returns to. 1.00 is the old framing, where one world pixel is one screen pixel. Below 1.00 the camera pulls back and more of the floor fits on screen.

The field of view and the light circles keep their size ON SCREEN when you move this row, so the lit picture looks the same and simply covers more world. The camera also stops at the edge of the painted art, so you never see the black void outside the map.

4 · The light sources themselves yellow, and visible from far

These three rows answer a different question: not "what can the character see", but "does a lit candle read as LIT". The first two also appear on page 1 under the Torch shape.

Why the flame looked washed out. The warm colour of a light used to travel only in the darkness mesh, as a vertex colour multiplied by the darkness alpha. That alpha is near zero exactly in the MIDDLE of the light, where the darkness has already opened — so the tint cancelled itself where it mattered most, and survived only on the rim. The yellow now comes from an ADDITIVE disc drawn over each flame, which has no such problem.

5 · The blur, the highlight and the grey layer the full-screen pass

A full-screen OpenGL pass. It runs on the finished picture, so it also covers walls, floor and shadows. The two brothers are stamped out of the blur and always stay sharp.

The whole screen keeps its colour. Two things mark the field of view instead:

  • Focus. Only the cone and the foot circles are sharp. Everything else is blurred.
  • Highlight. Inside the field of view the brightness and the saturation go up a little. Both values MULTIPLY what is already on screen, so a corner with no light stays dark: the highlight points, it does not reveal.

The old black-and-white filter is still in the shader but it is OFF ("PB: forca do cinzento" = 0). Raise it to bring the grey back; the rows below it then apply again, and colour survives inside the field of view and wherever light reaches.

6 · Objects what disappears in the dark

Four separate switches, because the trade-off is not the same for each. A newspaper that shows only when you look at it is good; a barrel that vanishes while you push it is not. Hiding an object never blocks the interaction — the character can still use it.

Two things never follow these switches.

Candlesticks and stairs are always drawn. They are how the player reads the floor: the candlestick says what is already lit, the staircase says where the level ends. Someone who cannot see the stairs does not know where to go. They still get the blur and the grey from the post-processing when they are outside the field of view — they just never fade out.

The monster is the opposite. It is the only thing in the game that is not revealed by being near you. It appears when it is inside the field of view AND real light reaches it — a candle, the lighter in your hand. The proximity ramp does not count for it, and neither do the vision circles at the brothers' feet (those exist with the light off, so they would give it away in the dark). The little brother's power still reveals it through everything, because that silhouette is drawn by its own object.

How to tune it

  1. Start with the shape. Set "Cone: abertura", "Cone: alcance" and "Cone: dureza da borda" until the wedge looks right. Ignore the colour for now.
  2. Then the darkness. "Escuro ambiente" sets how much of the room survives outside the cone. "Cone sem luz: escuridao" sets how much the cone alone lifts.
  3. Then what light reveals. Walk to a candle. "Luz: alcance que revela" sets the size of the colour bubble; "Sem luz: ve ate" sets how far you see with no candle at all.
  4. Then the flames. "Tocha: brilho da chama" until a lit room is obviously not a dark one. "Luz: devolve a cor" says how much colour the pool of light gives back; "PB: realce das luzes" / "PB: halo das luzes" carry the lights that are too weak or too far to reach that.
  5. Last, the look. "Desfoque fora da visao" until the wedge separates from the rest of the screen, then "Visao: realce (brilho)" and "Visao: realce (saturacao)" until you always know where the character is looking. Leave "PB: forca do cinzento" at 0 unless you want the black-and-white pass back.
